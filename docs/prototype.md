# Treetop Trebuchet — local playable physics checkpoint

Author decisions recorded October 19, 2026. This is a developing test bed, not a calibrated simulation or finalized game balance. Run `npm ci`, `npm start`, then open `http://localhost:8080`; `npm test` runs the checks. No public deployment.

## Current authorized direction

- Early Fourth Age / Arcane, academy project at the Grandfather Tree. Launch from a **VERY high branch** into a **fixed authored map**; future multiple maps are confirmed, current work concerns one.
- Travel through Grandfather Tree branches/leaves, open-sky freefall, a long jungle-canopy descent, then forest-floor bouncing/sliding while horizontal momentum remains. The 300 m launch pivot, canopy height, platforms and bumpers are test dimensions, not world lore.
- **The whole launcher is physical:** beam, counterweight, sling/cradle, payload and finite-mass frame with branch contact. Player configures hardware and loads the critter; angle/speed emerge. No desired-angle control, auto-aim, random launch force or velocity multiplier.
- Power timing selects a mechanically reachable **cocked counterweight height**, hence actual gravitational potential energy. Highest allowed lift stores the most counterweight potential energy; it does not guarantee greatest range in every configuration.
- Short dotted trajectory guidance fades out before obstacles. It observes the same physical simulation; it does not tune the shot or reveal bounce outcomes.
- Eleven articulated volunteers: lizard (light/grippy), salamander (slippery), mouse, turtle (carapace-heavy), flexible snake, chipmunk, very rare flying squirrel, very heavy armadillo, **pygmy possum** and **pygmy marmoset** (now approved), frog. All sizes/masses/frictions are tunable gameplay proportions, not biological measurements.
- A volunteer queue reveals only the current and roughly next three; seedable stochastic selection is allowed. **Terrain never randomizes.** Enemy encounters vary from biome-specific pools in the tree, sky, canopy and forest floor.
- **Infinite health, harmless cartoon slapstick.** No injury/death mechanic. Pure physical launching is distinct from approved arcade post-release nudges. Flying squirrel: right dives, left climbs with lift/drag; no free infinite ascent.
- Exact core loop: launch critter / watch bounces / earn points → see bells and shop → customize with owned equipment and decide on special items. Points and bells remain separate; numerical awards/prices are explicitly prototype balance, not canon conversion.
- Continued local iteration, original procedural sounds/art and independent reviews are authorized. No paid assets, copied franchise/game assets or public deployment. No new story canon is inferred from test equipment or enemies.

## Main implementation

`src/articulated.js` uses pinned **Planck 1.5.1** from npm, with velocity/position iteration counts 20/12 and a fixed 1/600 s tick. The engine handles rigid body contacts, revolute hinges with limits, and **tension-only maximum-length rope joints**. It is not an exact or experimentally calibrated mechanics solver.

The A-frame is a dynamic 30 kg prototype body with feet and struts touching the fixed branch. It can slide, recoil, rotate or tip. Its axle is a physical revolute joint to the arm. A latch locks only the arm-to-frame angle while loading, without fixing the frame to the world. The uniform wooden beam's density, geometry and inertia change together; the counterweight is a finite circular fixture rigidly attached to its short end. Hinged counterweights remain a later hardware option.

The cradle is an original U-shaped rigid contact body suspended by two maximum-length straps. Individual critter parts have physical shapes and mass fractions; limited revolute joints connect them. Parts collide with cradle, frame/terrain and enemies; self-collision within one critter is disabled for stability. Snake is a seven-part chain, not an animated single projectile. Geometry and joints are simple original placeholders.

### Loading

A finite spring-damper **hand force**, with mass-aware stiffness `120*m`, damping `18*m` and force cap `max(80,60*m)` N, pulls the grabbed body point toward the pointer. It does not set position or derive an impulse from raw pointer velocity. Mouse and touch use Pointer Events; optional tap-to-hold keeps the same physical grip until a second tap. A small hit-target pad helps select tiny bodies without moving them.

Lift the critter over the cradle and release the grip. Gravity, actual body contacts and strap constraints determine seating and settling. There is **no radial seating projection, automatic identical pose, velocity cap or forced settling duration** in the main UI. The prior `src/loading.js` shortcut was removed. Different drag histories and waiting times can produce different poses, spins and shots; identical complete initial states reproduce. Unloaded critters can fall onto the branch and be picked up again; Reset restores the initial test shelf.

### Release and power — explicit approximations

The release pin is currently an **idealized geometric switch**: a descending relative cradle/beam angle crossing, with positive strap tension, removes the straps and disables cradle collisions. Critter positions, part velocities and spins are not reassigned. This instantly opens the support model; an actual articulated hook, flexible fabric pouch and opening motion are **not** simulated. There is no claim that this is the final full-physics release mechanism.

The meter now selects a reachable **target** angle on `theta=-pi+asin(f)`, bounded by branch clearance. A finite-torque revolute motor actually winds from rest, at up to 0.6 rad/s and 1800 N m default torque, with reaction on the dynamic frame. Work is solved torque times relative joint displacement; positive input and braking are tracked separately. At the reached angle the latch engages without assigning body poses or resetting velocity. An underpowered motor can stall; it cannot fake the selected height. Motor inputs are validated. The final ideal latch and contact/numerical losses remain approximations.

`src/hook-experiment.js` adds an **optional open planar C-link** contacting a physical peg, with two retained tension-only straps and continuing cradle collisions. Opening and payload departure arise from contacts rather than deleting straps. A closed ring trapped around a pin cannot escape in this 2D plane; the C-link is explicitly an analogue, not a solved 3D ring mechanism. Release observation requires 10 ms of unsupported separation, resets on re-contact, and does not change payload velocities. Its current geometry produces early, weak shots; it remains off by default. The connector contacts only the peg, while straps are massless and noncolliding. The baseline geometric switch above is still the playable release.

### Prediction and reproducibility

Prediction copies the complete engine body/joint state and overridden masses/inertias, then unlatches and advances an isolated world. The UI reconstructs the same canonical snapshot before the actual launch because engine serialization omits contact manifold/TOI history; direct continuation of a preexisting world can differ slightly from a restored one even with warm starting disabled. Tests therefore compare the actual canonical UI path. Snapshotting a live grip is rejected; preview requires hands off and zero post-flight control.

The hint is at most 0.8 s after release, fading before actual clone contact events or conservative authored-surface bounds. New random enemies are excluded from this opening 0.8 s window. It previews neither future enemy interactions nor control input. Loading continues live, so a hint can change until the actual latch release. Preview refresh is bounded to roughly twice a second; the render clock pauses excess time after long inactive frames rather than attempting unbounded catch-up.

### Post-launch systems

- Physics continues for all critter body parts, the frame, arm and cradle. Fixed platforms and original passive restitution bumpers form a small pachinko test layout. Restitution stays below one; collisions themselves are not speed multipliers.
- Normal critters get a small **explicit arcade horizontal force** from arrows/touch controls. Its translational work is reported separately. Squirrel controls now use actual body pitch, speed-dependent lift perpendicular to COM velocity, stall/broadside/induced drag and a force cap. Left flares and right dives by idealized membrane deflection; there is no pitch motor. The aerodynamic force cannot add translational energy. Aerodynamic coefficients are prototypes, not validated anatomy or a complete sailplane model. Glide balancing needs further playtesting.
- Seeded encounter recipes become **dynamic bodies** on activation. Their positions are not overwritten after collision. Spawned moving bodies bring their own externally supplied initial energy; this is recorded separately. Active body count is capped, and expired distant encounters are removed. Original beetles, moth/bird shapes, pods and fungi are harmless interaction placeholders.
- `src/economy.js` keeps independent prototype point/bell awards, idempotent run banking, shop purchases, owned/equipped hardware and physical parameter effects. Frame mass/foot grip, beam geometry/material mass, counterweight and sling upgrades affect real model parameters. No launch-velocity modifiers. Special items are still a reserved decision slot, not invented items.
- `src/audio.js` synthesizes original short launch/impact/coin/shop/enemy/gust cues. Gesture-only AudioContext start, voice/chatter limits, mute and volume. Subjective sound mix remains to be auditioned/refined; no ripped sounds or music.
- Optional reduced decorative motion follows OS preference initially. Simulation-essential movement remains. Saving progress is explicit opt-in local storage; no external data or site publish.

## Reference models, tests and limitations

`src/physics.js` retains the independently derived fixed-pivot taut-sling Lagrange model as a conservative reference, with RK4 and refined separation events. Its zero-tension separation policy is a **reference-model boundary**, not the current main sling behavior. `src/rope.js` adds an independently tested unilateral Cartesian cable model: slack stays attached; slack-to-taut contact is refined and transfers an inelastic pulling impulse; RATTLE-style position/velocity constraints never push. These are research/verification modules rather than the active articulated scene.

The suite checks analytic energy/scale identities, timestep convergence, rope stretch/force signs, impact energy, input work, varied reproducible loading, all eleven shape/mass/joint profiles, finite frame response, physically driven ragdoll contact loading, snapshot/prediction consistency, physical upgrades, queues, enemies, economy and bounded original audio. **Passing tests do not establish calibrated realism or finished game feel.** Contact losses and engine numerical losses are not yet fully separated. The independent reviewer found noticeable shot differences at 1/300, 1/600 and 1/1200 s; ongoing work should quantify and reduce these before strong accuracy claims.

Remaining high-value work: useful and stable mechanical hook/pouch opening, all-species end-to-end loading/stability sweeps, tight engine convergence budgets, fuller energy ledgers for contact/spawns/control, better mobile framing and body picking, audio listening/mix, and richer authored collision arrangements. Base motion and loading should stay visible and physical while art improves.

## Research and original-work boundary

- [Horsdal, *The swinging counterweight trebuchet: On scaling and optimization*](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.01306v1): coupled-body energy, inertia and release phases. We do not claim to reproduce the complete hinged-counterweight paper.
- [Horsdal et al., *Experiments on inner movement and ranges*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.19442): future experimental calibration reference; its data have not calibrated this test bed.
- [LAMMPS SHAKE/RATTLE documentation](https://docs.lammps.org/fix_shake.html): position/velocity constraint formulation used as a numerical-method reference, not a claim that LAMMPS is installed or used here.
- [Planck rope joints](https://piqnt.com/planck.js/docs/joint/rope-joint.html), [limited revolute joints](https://piqnt.com/planck.js/docs/joint/revolute-joint.html), [mouse-joint discussion](https://piqnt.com/planck.js/docs/joint/mouse-joint.html): engine constraint APIs and finite-force interaction cautions.
- [*Toss the Turtle*, creator-published Newgrounds page](https://www.newgrounds.com/portal/view/508440): author's launch/flight-control/cash-upgrade loop informs pace and feedback. Its cannons, bombs, copyrighted characters, injury presentation and assets are not imported. Our hardware upgrades change mechanics; our slapstick is harmless.

## Iteration 3: measured limits and reaction forces

Axle damping now uses **relative arm/frame angular velocity** and applies equal-and-opposite torques. Its measured work uses relative joint displacement. Deliberate axle damping (`dampingWork`) and critter drag (`dragWork`) are saved separately from hand, motor and arcade work. The unaccounted many-body energy remainder must still be called a **mixed contact/constraint/numerical residual**, not pure numerical drift.

`node scripts/measure-losses.js` regenerates [isolated diagnostics](reviews/iteration-3/loss-diagnostics.json). A freely falling isolated body has −0.005039/−0.002519/−0.001260 J drift after one second at 300/600/1200 Hz, matching semi-implicit Euler's analytic drift and halving with timestep. A single frictionless wall impact at restitution 0, 0.5 and 1 loses the expected `(1-e²)` fraction of kinetic energy at each timestep. This separates two known effects in controlled worlds; it does **not** subtract either blindly from the articulated launcher's coupled contacts.

`node scripts/measure-species.js` regenerates the [all-species sweep](reviews/iteration-3/species-measurements.json). The unchanged finite-force loading recipe succeeds for **10/11** species and gives baseline pin-release center speeds about 18.6–27.8 m/s. The heavy armadillo fails seating under that recipe and capped hand force; no fallback snapping or velocity rescue is used. This is a reproducible scripted loading check, not proof that every human drag succeeds or that all limbs have cleared the cradle at the pin event.

The planar hook now validates contiguous C-link geometry, real mouth/peg clearance and solid-arc strap attachment. Contact-only release tagging is tested to preserve velocities and fixtures, and canonical cloned prediction agrees with launch. Bounded geometry/configuration sweeps remain in the [hook measurements](reviews/iteration-3/hook-measurements.json). These are experiments, not automatic per-shot tuning. The connector is still not a reliable useful replacement across hardware and masses, so it remains optional.

Mobile source checks fixed narrow landscape selection (640×360 now gets the wide canvas), portrait/landscape/portrait coordinate continuity and shrinking hardware controls. **Actual browser touch QA remains unverified:** the Mac reported locked, and the existing game tab's debugger was detached. The responsive override request and reset both failed before confirmation. No export retry was attempted. Audible audio QA also remains unverified.

Glide balance regression exposes rather than conceals the current limitation: from 100 m at 15 m/s and stable −0.2 rad pitch, neutral reaches roughly 340 m, held dive 237 m, held flare 37 m (stall); imposed 3 rad/s tumble gives only 26 m. Controls change idealized membrane incidence, not body pitch. A physical aerodynamic pitching moment or explicit actuator with rotational-work accounting is needed before claiming recoverable player-controlled orientation. No artificial stabilization was added.

Iteration-3 integrated verification: **91 tests pass**. The bounded hook sweep has 24 angle/span/attachment trials, of which only two throw forward/upward. Best measured hook speed is 4.686 m/s versus baseline 27.736 m/s; at 300/600/1200 Hz the selected hook gives 5.112/4.686/5.728 m/s. A heavier counterweight throws backward and a longer beam fails to release within five seconds. These results rule out promoting this connector as the default. Reproduce with `node scripts/measure-hook.js`; detailed comparable parameters and failures are retained. [Current launcher measurements](reviews/iteration-3/physics-measurements.json) also include a deliberately damped/dragged case with the known work terms separated from the remaining mixed residual.


## Iteration 4: heavy loading and passive attitude recovery

The grip now scales finite external effort with **total rig mass** rather than leaving a five-kilogram armadillo with the light rig's spring stiffness. It applies force at the actual grabbed body point and records supplied hand work. There is still no position assignment, gravity cancellation, velocity cap or seating snap. The armadillo's force cap is 300 N; light rigs keep at least 80 N. The unchanged timed target recipe now seats/releases **11/11 rigs**. Armadillo additionally stays seated after another second without the hand and matches cloned prediction. [Loading measurements](reviews/iteration-4/loading-measurements.json) exercise three hardware configs at 300/600/1200 Hz; all nine physically load with settled COM speeds below 0.19 m/s. Separate canonical launch snapshots keep loading history identical for timestep comparisons: 600→1200 Hz release velocity differences are roughly 0.139, 0.074 and 0.121 m/s for stock, heavier-counterweight and longer-beam setups respectively. These are representative tested bounds, not full solver calibration.

Squirrel aerodynamics now include a small trailing control membrane evaluated in **local airflow including angular velocity**. Both force and torque act on the same torso whose velocity/pitch defines the calculation. This is a torso-equivalent membrane model, not a claim of resolved flexible membranes. Its combined `F·v + torque*omega` power is nonpositive; pitch recovery trades existing kinetic/potential energy rather than using a powered attitude motor. Input changes bounded tail incidence. Work accounting includes rotational displacement. [Actual articulated measurements](reviews/iteration-4/articulated-glide.json) start from one physically loaded launch snapshot with approximately −8.90 rad/s torso spin; after 20 seconds its magnitude is below 0.014 rad/s in all nine input/timestep trials, with negative aerodynamic work. Flare remains higher/slower, dive lower/faster. At 600 Hz their 20-second horizontal distances are approximately 168.6/186.2/242.0 m for flare/neutral/dive. The neutral altitude differs about 2.5 m between 600 and 1200 Hz, so this long-horizon ragdoll/contact model is not tightly converged even though the isolated aerodynamic tests converge more closely.

The optional release checkbox now selects a **finite open-ended channel and sliding bead**, rather than the weak C-link. Two real rails and a closed heel are fixtures on the arm; a 0.04 kg, 0.05 m diameter bead supports the free strap and can physically exit the open tip. Geometry is shared across masses: 3.4 rad channel angle, 0.22 m length, 0.07 m internal width, friction 0.03. No slot joint, trigger impulse, release-time contact masking or strap deletion is used. `hardwareHasEscaped()` observes actual arm-local axial position past the mouth, accounting for bead radius and configured length. Payload release observes real loss of support over 10 ms; it may leave over the rim before hardware escape, without changing dynamics.

[Channel measurements](reviews/iteration-4/hook-measurements.json) show stock lizard speed16.32 m/s, turtle9.47, heavier counterweight18.68, shorter beam19.11 and longer beam11.75. The armadillo releases at about5.11 m/s but slightly backward, so the mechanism **is still not robust enough to replace the default**. All tested masses can now be loaded; the remaining failure concerns launch direction/efficiency. Across 300/600/1200 Hz stock lizard speeds are16.376/16.323/16.314 m/s, while 600→1200 Hz full velocity-vector difference is about0.456 m/s and release timing differs0.005 s. Similar scalar speeds alone do not prove tight vector convergence. Nine shared-angle cross-mass checks find no universal forward/upward setting. A split hinged-floor experiment failed stable loading/useful throws and remains an unexposed research flag. The baseline stays playable and the channel remains clearly optional.

Integrated iteration-4 checks: **99 passing tests**, including physical armadillo seating/unheld settling, three-hardware canonical timestep comparison, passive articulated squirrel tumble recovery, power-identity tests over random airflow/spin states, channel material/strap persistence, axial exit and canonical prediction consistency. Mac/browser visual and audible QA remain blocked as previously recorded; no export retry, push or deployment.

## Iteration 5: visible setups, posture sensitivity and honest feedback

The [contact-settled matrix](reviews/iteration-5/hook-matrix.json) and [mechanical analysis](reviews/iteration-5/hook-analysis.md) establish useful **explicit hardware examples**, without selecting them automatically by species. Light: M100/beam3/sling2.5/rail3.4rad/length0.22. Medium: M200/beam3/sling2.5/rail3.6/length0.12. Heavy: M150/beam2.2/sling2.2/rail3.8/length0.22. Short beam remains0.8 m. All three fixed loaded reference snapshots throw forward/upward and clear the launch branch at300/600/1200 Hz; first world contact is ground. Actual motor→loading→observed-contact trials also clear it. Fine-step velocity differences are approximately0.20/0.06/0.41 m/s, with larger coarse heavy differences. Net launch energy falls without explicit input work, while small per-step correction gains remain disclosed.

However, [additional unheld-wait trials](reviews/iteration-5/loading-variation.json) expose insufficient robustness. The light load can fall back onto the branch; the heavy load can throw backward/downward as its articulated posture redistributes. A small COM-relative speed does not mean every part or the whole cradle is stationary: the heavy failed pose is head-supported and high in the cup, with other parts moving; the successful pose has lower COM and more support. The default heavy backward sign persists across timesteps, supporting a mechanical configuration/posture explanation rather than a simple unstable numerical sign, but this does not validate exact contact fidelity. **The physical mode is not promoted to default.** No waiting timer, reset, snap, velocity correction or hidden per-species tuning masks this behavior.

The lab now exposes actual sling length, rail mounting and rail length, plus a table of measured examples. These change geometry, not desired launch angle. Equipped shop hardware takes precedence over matching lab dimensions, as disclosed in the UI. Read-only loading diagnostics report actual cradle contacts, whole-cradle speed/spin, individual-part relative motion and spin in addition to COM motion. The playable UI requires actual cradle contact before launching, still permits moving contact, clears hints when contact/eligibility is lost or winding resumes, and shows backward/no-separation/moving-load feedback. Clone serialization still omits contact manifolds; the UI verifies contact on the live source before canonical reconstruction, while the low-level scene API retains geometric eligibility for reproducible reconstruction.

Reproduce with `node scripts/measure-hook.js --matrix` and `node scripts/measure-release-variation.js`. The extra quiet-window observations in these diagnostic scripts only advance ordinary physics; they add no forces. **102 tests pass** at this checkpoint. Next mechanism work is a split pouch with passive hinge travel stops, derived and tested so that actual free-strap escape can open it rather than relying primarily on posture-sensitive rim ejection. Browser/audio QA remains blocked; no export retry or deployment.
