# Close-up loading workshop

The current starter is 1.2 m to the axle with a 1.04 m / 0.26 m lever, 0.96 m sling, 6,000 g hinged ballast, 20 mm oak beam and 1.6 m guide rail. It retains the 150 g capacity and three small starter volunteers. The mouse benchmark releases at 39.08° / 22.00 m/s; the chipmunk at 59.28° / 14.58 m/s. Larger preset recipes keep the preceding tower dimensions. Only untouched old starter recipes migrate (including the earlier free geared-wheel variant, while retaining that wheel); custom geometry, purchased parts, Bells and saved blueprints are retained.

The beam rests higher before winding, giving the launch view a taller silhouette. Setup frames it at roughly 40–50% of viewport height, bounded by the available width in portrait, with ground at 77% height. The stand is physically to the left of the machine. Animals, frame, stand and ropes share one metre scale. Flight still follows the actual released capsule.

Loading has its own Planck preparation world in similarity units. Three mass-weighted, joint-limited dynamic regions represent the body, head and tail/haunch. The existing vector critter artwork is clipped across those moving regions; no human operator is introduced. A kinematic pointer handle and revolute pin preserve the exact picked point, so an off-centre grab rotates and swings the volunteer. Pointer movement changes the handle, not animal transforms. On release the pin disappears, leaving gravity, damping and physical stand/branch/basket contacts. For front-accessible placement the basket is a sensor while held and becomes solid on drop. Real basket contact and low settling speed are required before seating; a mere nearby cursor does not load it. Off-screen misses return to the stand; cancellation returns safely without firing.

Seating deliberately transfers at rest into the existing single-capsule launch model, with zero loading velocity. It is a jointed loading interaction, not a full articulated flight ragdoll or deformable cloth basket. A manual missed drop can be picked up again. Enter explicitly seats for keyboard play; Space winds/releases. Pauses, dialogs, blur and hidden tabs do not advance preparation or fire. No arbitrary hand throw becomes trebuchet launch energy.

The visible hauling cord joins the wheel drum, frame-mounted guide pulley and sling basket. Drum travel is derived from the shortening free span. Clockwise winds; the counterweight/arm motion drives payout. Unlatch disengages the hauling hook, so the line cannot tether the released volunteer. Winding remains supported, quasi-static placement with gravitational lifting work and motor power limits; this is not an elastic cable or simulated gear-contact drivetrain. The original flexible sling strands and guide rail remain.

A looping dotted path and cursor-paw drag gesture appears after five active idle seconds, and immediately after an empty-launch attempt. The message is “You need something to launch!” Empty attempts stay ready and do not produce a result. The gesture stops on pickup or seating and respects paused/reduced-motion presentation. Idle solver bodies sleep; active hints or input redraw, and nearby scenery keeps its existing render budgets.
